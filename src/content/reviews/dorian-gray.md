The preface is a dare, and then the book spends the rest of its time showing off. I let it. Lord Henry’s lines are awful and *quotable*, which is the trap. I copied two of them down before I noticed I was agreeing, and then I crossed them out.

Dorian himself is less interesting than the idea of him, until the painting starts doing the aging for him. I loved the nastiness of ||Dorian killing Basil, the man who painted him||. It is melodramatic. I did not care. I wanted the portrait punished, and I wanted Dorian punished, and the book was happy to do both.

**Beautiful people getting away with things** is the itch this novel scratches and then infects. I closed it and felt slightly vain about my own face in the dark window, which I am choosing to take as the point.
