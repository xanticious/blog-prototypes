I forgot how *angry* the opening is. Lowood is cold, and Jane narrates it like someone who has already decided she will not be grateful. **That voice is the whole book.** I would follow it into a worse house than Thornfield.

The middle is the part I dog-eared. Rochester is rude and funny and too much, and Jane matches him sentence for sentence until she doesn’t. I knew something was wrong when ||the wedding stopped because Rochester already had a wife||. I had guessed, and I still flinched.

I would hand this to someone who asked me for romantasy and then tell them it was published in 1847. The brooding house, the secret, the woman who leaves anyway: the shape is already here.

:::spoiler
Bertha is the wife in the attic. The fire comes later, Rochester is injured, and Jane returns with money of her own. “Reader, I married him” still makes me laugh, because she has earned the right to be that blunt.
:::
