The *Odyssey* does not start with the hero. It starts with a council of gods and a son who is tired of hosting his mother’s suitors. Telemachus is roughly twenty. His father has been missing since he was a baby. The house is being eaten in public. Athena, in disguise, nudges him out of the hall and into a journey of his own. Homer’s structure is a kindness to the latecomer: before we follow Odysseus from cave to ship to shore, we understand what his absence costs.

When Odysseus does appear, he is sitting on a beach, crying, trapped on an island by a goddess who offers him immortality and is baffled that he wants Ithaca instead. The choice is the poem’s thesis. Home, in this story, is not a soft word. It is a rocky island, a marriage under strain, a father in a hut, a dog on a dunghill, a contest with a bow that has been waiting.

## Stories inside the story

Much of what people call the adventure — the Cyclops, the bag of winds, the Sirens, the cattle of the sun — is told by Odysseus himself, after dinner, to the Phaeacians who will finally give him a ride. He is a narrator with something to gain. Homer lets us enjoy the tales and also notice the performance. Odysseus is polytropos, a man of many turns: clever, loyal, ruthless, curious, willing to lie when a lie will get him through a door. The poem does not sand him into a mascot. The Cyclops is blinded. The suitors will be killed in a hall that becomes a slaughterhouse. Hospitality is sacred here precisely because the world is dangerous enough to require a law.

The best of those laws is xenia, the guest-host bond. Again and again, character is revealed by how a stranger is fed. The Phaeacians are almost comically good at it. Polyphemus is a parody of a host. Penelope’s suitors are guests who will not leave, which is its own kind of violence. If you read the poem watching the meals, the ethics get very clear.

> “There is a time for many words, and there is also a time for sleep.”

## Recognition, not just return

The second half of the poem, back on Ithaca, is slower and, for many readers, deeper. Odysseus comes home as a beggar. He tests people before he reveals himself. Some readers find the testing cold. It is also a theory of knowledge: after twenty years, recognition has to be earned by a sign, a scar, a bed built around a living tree that cannot be moved. Penelope is his equal in caution. She does not collapse into his arms because a man with the right story walked in. She asks a question only the builder of their bed could answer. Eurycleia, the nurse, knows him by a scar from a boar hunt and is told to be quiet. The dog Argos knows him and dies. Homer can break your heart in ten lines and then return to the tactics of a fight.

## Where a new reader can stand

The poem is three movements, and the middle one is the famous one:

1. Telemachus, learning what his father’s absence costs.
2. Odysseus, telling his own **sea-monsters** to people who may not believe him.
3. Ithaca, where coming home is a series of tests.

Translations differ so much that choosing one is part of the reading. A plainer contemporary English will show you the plot and the hospitality scenes. A more formal line will remind you that this was song. Either way, do not skip the Telemachy at the start, and do not rush the recognitions at the end in order to get back to the sea monsters. The monsters are the part everyone already knows. The poem’s rarer gift is the difficulty of coming home and being believed.
